# Personal Effectiveness
- 2 Fundamental rules: do 1 thing, make sure it's the right thing

## Rule 1: do 1 thing
- Context Switching: literally hitler

- Deep vs shallow work
  - Deep Work: the resistance movement
  - Shallow Work: the occupying force
  - Deep Work is both rare and valuable and must be protected
  - Deep work requires uninterrupted periods of focus
  - Quantifying depth: how long would it take for a recent college graduate?

- Preventing context switching
  - Your phone: the Battle of the Somme
    - Remove distracting apps
    - Use a Work Mode
    - Put it away out of sight
  - Email and instant messaging: the Ardennes Offensive
    - CLOSE these apps when working
    - Indicate to yourself when you are doing deep work
    - Don't allow yourself to touch anything else in that time
  - Open office: the Gallipoli Campaign
    - Use and respect the red sliders
    - Get a noise-cancelling headphone or simply ear plugs if you need to
  - Algorithmically cured content: the Battle of Stalingrad
    - Short-form content has been shown to reduce attention span
    - It is highly addictive
    - It transforms your phone from a boring device into something enticing
    - Even outside of work: if you spend your downtime scrolling, you will not recover as well
    - You need to spend time without being stimulated to properly recharge

- Doing Deep Work
  - Treat a deep work session like going to the movies
  - Lead Measures: timer helps
  - Cadence of accountability: will revisit later (review and planning)
  - Disconnecting from work
    - Downtime aids insight
    - Downtime helps recharge
    - Work that downtime replaces is usually not good/important
    - Implement a shutdown ritual
  - Embrace boredom
    - Productive breaks: walk, do something. No phone!
    - Mindfulness/meditation
    - Do things without external stimuli
  - Timeblock planning
    - Block deep work sessions as if they are important meetings
    - Respect a shallow work budget
    - Set a time when you stop working and work backwards

## Rule 2: make sure it's the right thing
- Multiscale planning and review
  - Quarterly: spend a good chunk of time (half day) setting goals for this quarter
  - Weekly: end/begin your week with a quick review of the last week and planning of the next, using your quarterly plan
  - Daily: have a shutdown ritual where you plan what to do on the next day
  - Result: you can always focus on the current thing without having to worry about the bigger picture

- Full Capture (Getting Things Done)
  - ...

- Taking notes
  - Obsidian: local, markdown files, plugins available
  - Notion: cloud, more powerful out of the box, less control over data
  - Most important concept: linking and backlinking!
  - Keep a (limited) research diary and keep a personal "wiki" ("second brain")
    - When you need to write, you won't start from a blank page

- Cognitive fitness
  - Focusing is not pleasant, it's tempting to avoid it
  - We always tend to take the path of least resistance
  - Invest in your knowledge portfolio (pragmatic programmer topic 6)
  - Examples
    - Procrastinotion
      - I have a system that makes me feel in control
      - I spend a lot of time tweaking my Notion dashboard as a kind of hobby
      - My system is so complex and intricate it introduces friction
    - AI coding
      - I did the thing in less time using AI
      - I did some extra, perhaps unnecessary things in the same amt of time
      - I used AI for procrastinotion
    - High school picture: studying for math without doing => fail
      - This can be AI, but also existed before AI
      - In general: you need to DO to LEARN
